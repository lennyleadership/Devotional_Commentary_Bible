<div id="devotional" class="tabcontent">

The peace offering is a real pattern for the Lord’s Supper, not a verse-by-verse prophecy of it. I’ll check how tightly the New Testament actually draws that line.

Yes. The peace offering is a pattern of the Lord’s Supper, not a prophecy of it.

Leviticus never mentions the cross, the bread, or the cup. What it gives is a sequence: blood is applied, the best portion goes to God, and then clean people eat. The New Testament treats that sequence as <b>fulfilled</b>, not repeated.

What Paul actually uses  
The clearest link is 1 Corinthians 10:16–18. Paul says the cup is a sharing in Christ’s blood and the bread is a sharing in Christ’s body. He then points to Israel: those who ate the sacrifices were sharing in the altar.

The Lord’s Supper corresponds to that meal at three points:

The altar comes before the table. Peace is enjoyed because a sacrifice has already been accepted.

God, the priest, and the people share one offering. The church shares one Christ.

The meal is holy. Unclean eating was cut off in Israel; eating without discerning the body brings judgment in Corinth.

Passover is also a pattern, and it is the nearer one for the night Jesus instituted the Supper. The peace offering supplies the fellowship-meal logic. Neither text says, “This is the Lord’s Supper.”

The table does not offer Christ again. It is a meal on a sacrifice finished at the cross, and a proclamation of that death until He comes.

血先归给神，然后才吃饭  
利未记3章里，血要洒在坛上，脂油要烧掉，然后献祭的人才能吃肉。筵席不是用来换平安，而是承认平安已经因祭物而有了。利未记7章还规定，感谢祭的肉必须当天吃完，不能把这顿饭从祭坛上拆下来，变成普通晚餐。

主的桌子也是这样。饼和杯不是新的祭。基督已经一次献上。教会吃喝，是因为血已经流出，最好的部分已经归给神。

保罗把两张桌子连在一起  
保罗没有引用利未记3章的章节，但他<b>用以色列人吃祭物来解释主餐</b>：

我们所祝福的杯，岂不是同领基督的血吗？我们所擘开的饼，岂不是同领基督的身体吗？……你们看属肉体的以色列人，那吃祭物的，不就是同领坛上的祭物吗？（哥林多前书10:16–18）

在五种祭里，只有平安祭让献祭的人吃祭肉。祭司吃的是圣所里的份。吃平安祭的人，是在与坛上的祭有份。保罗说，喝这杯、吃这饼，就是与基督的血和身体有份。所以主的桌子是平安祭筵席的应验，不是把基督再献一次。

Leviticus 7:19 is the verse that says who may eat it: “As for the flesh, everyone who is clean may eat of it.”

Why Israel was given this offering  
Leviticus 7:11–21 gives three reasons, and none of them is payment for sin.

A freewill gift, to praise God for kindness that was not even asked for.

A vow, to keep a promise made in trouble once God had answered.

Thanksgiving for deliverance.

The fat was burned for the Lord. The priest received a portion. The worshiper and other clean people ate the rest. The meal said, “Peace with God is real, and we may sit at His table.” It was a response to grace, not a way to buy it.


Christians are commanded to remember Jesus’ death, but the command is attached to the Lord’s Supper, not to the peace offering.

The peace offering helps you understand that table. Israel ate only after blood had been given and the best portion had gone to God. The church eats because Christ’s body has been given and His blood has sealed the new covenant. The meal does not repeat the sacrifice. It remembers and proclaims a death that is already finished.

God gave Israel the peace offering so they could thank Him and share a holy meal. God gave the church the Lord’s table so we would remember, and proclaim, the death of Jesus until He comes. The first is the pattern. The second is the command.
